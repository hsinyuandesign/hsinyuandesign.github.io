01 Overview

Khan Academy runs almost entirely on donations and volunteers. That makes the donation flow one of the most important screens on the entire site.

However, it wasn't pulling its weight. Why does the current donation flow feel like an afterthought instead of a place people want to give to?

Our team redesigned everything in the donation flow from the first step of choosing the donation amount to visualizing real outcomes and leading with gratitude, rebuilding trust one step at a time.

This redesign focuses on first-time and hesitant donors — the group most likely to abandon the flow before giving at all.

04 Process

We explored different ideation directions, iterating on structure and flow. Any idea that didn't map to the previous four pillars were cut, converging on our final iterations .

Here are some of the iterations I went through designing the amount and frequency step — the first real decision a donor makes on the page.

Ultimately, we chose to prioritize the smallest possible ask with the clearest possible impact — a one-time default donations paired with amounts tied to real outcomes, even knowing Khan Academy loses some of the predictability recurring gifts provide.

V1:Recurring kept as default, with "cancel anytime, no commitment" reassurance copy added.
Softens the ask, but doesn't remove the barrier.
V2:Blank "Enter amount" field, one-time donations pre-selected as default.
Flexible + lower barrier, but forces donors to decide with no clear outcome shown.
V3:One-time selected as default, preset tap-to-select amounts, each paired with an outcome.
Removes the ask before it's made, and removes the guesswork — amount and outcome visible together.

05 Solution

Default to one-time, not recurring

Recurring was the default. That's a big ask before someone's even given once.

We flipped it — one-time first, with donation amounts tied to real impact, so donors could see exactly what their gift meant and did.

Before:Recurring by default
After:One-time by default, tied to impact
Before:Thank you diluted by the immediate receipt
After:Thank you gets its own moment

Leading with gratitude, not a receipt

Donors used to land on a cold confirmation number the second they gave: now, we gave gratitude its own moment.

The confetti animation further makes the impact feel real and enhances the donor’s feeling of accomplishment. The receipt is still there, just no longer sharing the spotlight.

The Learners Fund, reimagined

The Learner's Fund is Khan Academy's annual donor community — originally linked on the donation page, competing with a donor's first decision.

We moved it after the thank you and added suggested amounts, so nothing competes with that first ask. This means donors who already want to give annually take one extra step to find it — a tradeoff we made for donors who arrive still deciding.

Before:Learner's Fund competes with the default donation
After:Learner's Fund offered after the thank you, one ask at a time

06 Reflection

🪙 Generosity is a UX problem before it’s a money problem.

In this redesign, we didn’t focus on reducing the default donation amount, as we knew money was likely not the problem. The real drop-off wasn't friction — it was doubt. Donors weren't unsure how to give, they were unsure their $10 mattered.

I learned that order changes meaning. Moving the thank you before the receipt didn't add a feature, it changed how giving felt — from a transaction to a moment of gratitude.
