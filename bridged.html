01 Overview

This project came out of a 3 day designathon prompt: "Bridging generational gaps through design."

We were asked how design might help younger and older generations learn from one another and build meaningful relationships.

Social platforms aren't usually built for that kind of exchange. They reward fast, performative posts, not reflection. So my team set out to design something different: a prompt-based storytelling experience.

I worked on the full arc of that exchange, from the questions that get someone to open up, to the personal timeline where their perspectives and stories gets a place to live, be found, and be related to by another stranger.

02 Problem

Generational gaps rarely come from disagreement, but come from never really having the chance to listen.

Younger people feel like older generations don't get modern challenges. Older generations feel left behind by fast-changing technology. Neither side is wrong, they just never got the chance to hear each other out.

03 Research

We interviewed across generations, ran peer discussions, and surveyed 60+ people.

One of our key findings:

Both generations also wanted to learn and teach each other on a lot of the same few topics, from life outlook to mental health, showing the exchange wasn't one-directional.

These insights shaped everything: people want to connect. What's missing is a structure that makes sharing feel safe.

Through more primary & secondary research, we saw that storytelling offers this safe space. It's how humans have always passed down values and found common ground. It doesn't require agreement, just attention.

04 Process

Making storytelling more accessible.

My first instinct was to design daily prompts, so no one had to figure out what was worth sharing on their own.

So we designed for open reflection first and foremost, with prompts still available as an extra option.

I spent most of my time iterating the onboarding flow, to give users the opportunity to tell their own story with structure.

Here are some of the iterations I went through when designing onboarding: what gets a user from a blank page to their first story.

I started by brainstorming features I wanted to add, from optional photos to emotion tags, then iterated based on feedback from mentors and users.

V1:Our very first iteration, with everything we thought a user needed to answer to tell a story.
Made it easier for users to start, but felt more like a form than a reflection.
V2:Added progress indicator and a more consistent design system.
Onboarding broken into steps, reducing cognitive load; fields reframed as questions instead of labels like "Date."
V3:Reordered flow, with feelings on the event asked after the story.
This matches how memory actually works, as people only know how they felt once they've reflected. Voice input and response feedback added to reduce friction and encourage depth.

05 Solution

A story behind an opinion

Users document milestones, giving context behind prompt responses instead of just the perspective itself. This lets both generations see how their unique experiences have shaped their thoughts and who they are.

A milestone entry, with context behind each prompt response
Optional photos, emoji tags, and gentle prompts to encourage a fuller entry

Sharing that's expressive, not performative

Optional photos, emoji-based emotion tags, and a simple chronological layout kept storytelling lightweight and human, without pressure to perform. A soft prompt ("Go a little deeper") encourages a fuller entry.

A daily prompt system

One guided question a day, sparking accessible storytelling across generations, for the days a blank page feels like too much.

06 Reflection

🧓 Generational divides are context gaps, not value conflicts.

Design can't force two generations to agree. However, what it can do is surface the context that was missing. And that turned out to be enough to shift the conversation from debate over to empathy.

We didn't place in the designathon, but the process taught me so much, like how to turn a research insight into a real design, especially under pressure.

If I revisited this, I'd spend even more time on the onboarding process, especially for older users who need a gentler on-ramp than a 3 day sprint allowed us to design.

This was my first designathon, and my first real taste of high-stakes collaborative design work. I can't wait to do it again next year!
